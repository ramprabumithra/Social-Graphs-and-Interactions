ONE UNIVERSE,
26 EXCEPTIONS

303 Marvel characters, one Wikipedia snapshot, and the handful who share no link with anyone else in it.

ISSUE №1 · BY [YOUR GROUP NAME] · [NAME 1], [NAME 2], [NAME 3]

Every character in Wikipedia's Marvel Comics superheroes category, and every internal link between their pages, arrives this week as a single frozen snapshot — 303 nodes, 1,784 directed edges. Our job was to turn that raw edge list into a first real picture: who gets linked to, who does the linking, and who — improbably, in a shared universe — ends up connected to no one at all.

01What we asked

The dataset splits every relationship into a direction: character A's article links to character B's, but B doesn't have to link back. That asymmetry seemed like the most interesting thread to pull on. Concretely: does getting linked to behave differently from doing the linking? And structurally — with 303 characters supposedly sharing one universe, is the graph actually one connected mass, or does it fracture into pieces the category page doesn't admit to?

02Fame has no ceiling. Effort does.

Spider-Man is linked to by 106 other characters' articles — by far the highest in-degree in the network, and roughly a third of the entire cast. But Spider-Man doesn't hold the record for linking out: that belongs to Betsy Braddock, whose article mentions 28 other characters, less than a third of Spider-Man's in-degree.

That gap isn't noise, it's structural. An in-link is a decision made by someone else — any of the other 302 articles can choose to mention you, and there's no upper bound on how many will. An out-link has to be physically written into one page, and no editor writes an article with hundreds of outbound mentions. One side of this relationship scales with the size of the whole encyclopedia; the other scales with how much one person felt like typing.

03The island nobody remembers

Strip out direction and just ask who's connected to whom at all, and the network breaks into 19 separate pieces. One giant component holds 277 characters — 91% of the cast, everyone from Spider-Man to Frankenstein's Monster's Marvel Comics counterpart. Then there are 17 characters with zero links in either direction: Baymax, Super Rabbit, and fifteen others nobody thought to mention and who didn't mention anyone back.

The genuine surprise was the piece in between: a self-contained cluster of exactly 9 characters, fully disconnected from the other 294. Every one of them turned out to belong to the same obscure Marvel team — the Morituri — a squad none of us had heard of before this exercise, floating in its own small corner of the wiki with no bridge back to the Avengers-and-friends mainstream.

WHAT SURPRISED US

We expected the isolates — obscure one-off characters are exactly what you'd expect to find alone. What we didn't expect was a clique sitting completely outside the main universe: nine characters who all know each other but who the rest of the Marvel wiki has entirely forgotten. It's a reminder that "connected to the network" and "connected to each other" are different questions, and the edge-harvesting method (running text only, not shared navboxes) is honest enough to let a real narrative island show up rather than papering over it with templated links.

04Method note

Built from week1_nodes.tsv and week1_edges.tsv, loaded as a directed graph with every node added before the edges (so the 17 isolates survive). Degree distributions computed on raw counts, no binning — this week's dataset is small enough that the raw dots are still legible. Layout is NetworkX's spring layout; node size in Fig. 2 scales with undirected degree.

DEGREES OF SEPARATION

Pick any two characters. BFS runs live in your browser and finds the shortest chain of Wikipedia links between them.

TRY:

How this works

The graph is the same week-1 snapshot from the course data page — 303 characters, 1,784 directed Wikipedia links, loaded straight into your browser as JSON. Pressing "Find the Path" builds an adjacency list on the spot and runs breadth-first search from the starting character, which guarantees the shortest possible chain (BFS explores one hop at a time, so the first time it reaches the target is provably the shortest route). By default the search ignores link direction — treating "A links to B" the same as "B links to A" — since that's what makes a six-degrees-style search meaningful for a directed, sparse wiki graph. Tick the box above to restrict it to real directed edges instead, and watch some paths get longer or disappear entirely.

Not every pair is connected. 17 characters (like Baymax) have zero links in either direction, and a 9-character clique (the Morituri) is only connected to itself — no BFS, however patient, will find a bridge out.
